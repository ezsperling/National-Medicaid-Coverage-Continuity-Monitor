# National Medicaid Coverage Continuity Monitor

An exploratory Python Streamlit app for observed Medicaid and CHIP enrollment, renewal, and operational measures in all 50 states and DC. It also includes a simple enrollment forecast by state and program. Prepared for a Senior Manager, Product Performance portfolio conversation. It does not forecast the effect of Public Law 119-21 or score individual members. The earlier R Shiny prototype remains in `app.R` for reference.

## Get the source data yourself

1. Open [CMS State Medicaid and CHIP Applications, Eligibility Determinations, and Enrollment Data](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/6165f45b-ca93-5bb5-9d06-db29c692a360). Select **Data Table** and **Download full dataset (CSV)**; open **Data Dictionary** for column definitions. This is a **state × reporting month × preliminary/updated version** table. In the download checked September 24, 2026, there were 11,016 raw rows, covering September 2013 through May 2026. Columns include `State Abbreviation`, `Reporting Period` (YYYYMM), `Preliminary or Updated` (P/U), `State Expanded Medicaid` (Y/N), `Total Medicaid and CHIP Enrollment`, `Total Adult Medicaid Enrollment`, `Total Call Center Volume (Number of Calls)`, `Average Call Center Wait Time (Minutes)`, `Average Call Center Abandonment Rate`, and measure-specific footnotes. Some fields are blank; call center measures vary in coverage and definition.
2. Open [CMS State Medicaid and CHIP Eligibility Processing Data](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/5abea2e0-3f8e-4b49-a50d-d63d5fd9103c). Again use **Data Table → Download full dataset (CSV)** and **Data Dictionary**. This is a **state × reporting month × original/updated version** table. The checked download had 3,774 raw rows from March 2023 through May 2026. Key columns: `Beneficiaries with a Renewal Due`, `Beneficiaries Whose Coverage Was Renewed (Total)`, `Beneficiaries Whose Coverage Was Renewed on an Ex Parte Basis`, `Beneficiaries Disenrolled for Procedural Reasons at Renewal`, `Beneficiaries Determined Ineligible at Renewal`, `Beneficiaries with a Pending Renewal`, and corresponding footnotes. Do not treat ineligibility and procedural disenrollment as the same outcome.
3. For subgroup context, open [Major Eligibility Group Information by Month](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/ea9b7db3-db71-4663-b4e1-67e11d1d4fcc). Its grain is **state × month × eligibility group** (e.g., adult expansion, children, disability) and availability and quality differ by state and month. This is a *future extension*, not yet included in this app.
4. For enacted policy and implementation guidance, read [CMS Working Families Tax Cut Legislation](https://www.medicaid.gov/resources-for-states/working-families-tax-cut-legislation) and its [Community Engagement page](https://www.medicaid.gov/resources-for-states/working-families-tax-cut-legislation/community-engagement). This is **policy text and guidance, not a numeric member dataset**. Record provision, affected population, effective date, source URL, and review date separately. Do not infer that all Medicaid members are subject to community engagement.
5. For Medicare, use [CMS Medicare Monthly Enrollment](https://data.cms.gov/summary-statistics-on-beneficiary-enrollment/medicare-and-medicaid-reports/medicare-monthly-enrollment). The project uses state-level `TOT_BENES` and `DUAL_TOT_BENES` for May 2026. The data API ID and filtered request are recorded in `data/program_metadata.json`.
6. For managed LTSS, use [CMS Managed Long-Term Services and Supports (MLTSS) Enrollees](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/5394bcab-c748-5e4b-af07-b5bf77ed3aa3). This table is **annual, latest 2024 in the bundled snapshot**, and separates comprehensive managed care LTSS enrollees from MLTSS-only enrollees. Missing source cells stay missing. It does not cover all LTSS, including fee-for-service LTSS.

## How the data joins

`State Abbreviation` + `Reporting Period` joins the first two CSVs. Each source can have multiple versions for a state-month; the refresh script selects **U** (updated) over **P** (preliminary) for enrollment and **U** over **O** (original) for renewals when present. It retains the chosen version and footnotes. It includes 50 states and DC; territories are excluded. The produced file has **one row per state-month** with the fields below.

| Field in `cms_state_month.csv` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `state`, `state_name`, `month` | Join keys and display name; month is `YYYYMM` |
| `expanded` | Expansion status as reported for that month in the enrollment source |
| `enrollment`, `medicaid_enrollment`, `chip_enrollment`, `adult_enrollment` | CMS-reported combined and separate program enrollment counts; adult Medicaid is a subset |
| `renewal_due`, `renewed`, `procedural`, `ineligible`, `pending` | CMS renewal counts; keep the outcome categories separate |
| `call_volume`, `wait_minutes`, `abandon_rate` | Call center measures, which need footnote checks before comparison |
| `*_version`, `*_footnote` | Selected reporting version and source caveats, including separate Medicaid and CHIP enrollment notes |

The main rate is `procedural / renewal_due × 100`, calculated only when both values exist and `renewal_due > 0`. National rates use **sum(procedural) / sum(renewal_due)** over states reporting both, rather than averaging state percentages. This is the share of people *due for renewal* who were procedurally disenrolled, not the share of all disenrollments that were procedural. The app's enrollment map and table can separately show Medicaid and CHIP, but renewal outcomes in this file are not separated by program. May 2026 renewal data are original (O) reports and may be revised. Georgia and New York have denominator footnotes; eight states have procedural outcome footnotes. Inspect both in the app before comparing states. A state with a high rate is a lead for investigation, not evidence of low member literacy or policy causation. National reported enrollment should not be interpreted as an exact count of people subject to a specific future provision.

The **Investigation queue** compares a selected month with the same month one year earlier. It shows procedural rate and count changes, automatic renewals as a percentage of all people due, and pending renewals. Review CMS footnotes for both months before interpreting a change. Select a state to see a 30-month chart of automatic renewals, procedural outcomes, and pending renewals on a common denominator. This is descriptive and does not estimate causation. [California DHCS's January 2026 implementation plan, page 5](https://sbud.senate.ca.gov/media/3460) documents the July 2025 automatic renewal decline following expiration of unwinding flexibilities. [CMS's temporary-flexibilities guidance](https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/cib050924-e14.pdf) gives the federal timeline. These links are contextual evidence, not policy attribution for other states.

At the top of the app, **What changed in the CMS data?** compares the selected state and reporting month with the immediately preceding month, including Medicaid and CHIP enrollment separately. **What changed in state policy or operations?** shows dated events from `data/policy_events.csv` for the selected state and month, with source URLs. The register currently includes 15 states' documented procedural delay strategies for 2023 renewal cohorts, California July 2025 automatic renewal context, and California January 2026 asset limits; it is not a comprehensive policy chronology. `data/policy_history.csv` separately holds a CMS December 11, 2024 historical snapshot of approved renewal flexibilities for all 50 states and DC. The mostly empty all-state policy table has been replaced with selected-state verified evidence and direct CMS State Plan Amendment and waiver lookup links. An empty month means the project's research is incomplete, not that the state made no change. The nearby **Four program landscape** has a state product snapshot for Medicaid, CHIP, Medicare (where bundled), and annual managed LTSS. CMS renewal outcomes are statewide totals: this release cannot split procedural disenrollments into Medicaid, CHIP, Medicare, or LTSS products.

The policy panel also shows federal context for every jurisdiction: the [2024 eligibility and enrollment final rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/streamlining-medicaid-childrens-health-insurance-program-and-basic-health-program-application) and the [CMS November 2025 bulletin](https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/cib11182025.pdf) explaining which later-compliance provisions were paused by Public Law 119-21, plus the [June 2026 community engagement interim final rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicaid-community-engagement-requirement-certain-individuals-interim-final-rule-comment-period-cms) with generally 2027 implementation. Federal applicability does not show whether a state changed its operations in the selected reporting month or caused an observed rate change.

**Did CMS revise this month's renewal outcomes?** compares CMS original (O) and updated (U) rows for the same state and reporting month. `refresh_data.py` regenerates `data/renewal_revisions.csv` alongside the main snapshot. Updated rows exist only for reporting months CMS has revised; May 2026 has original reports only in this snapshot. The policy register records separate source publication, rule effective, state implementation, and affected-group fields when independently established. The 2023 procedural-delay source lists renewal cohort months rather than exact state implementation dates; those date fields remain blank.

## Run it

Install Python 3.10 or newer. In a terminal, open this project folder and run:

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m streamlit run app.py
```

The terminal displays a local URL, usually `http://localhost:8501`. The included CSVs are snapshots. To refresh from CMS, run `python refresh_data.py` and then `python refresh_programs.py` from this folder, then restart the app or clear its cache. An internet connection is needed for refresh but not for viewing the bundled snapshots. `data/metadata.json` and `data/program_metadata.json` record retrieval times and source IDs. Medicare is aligned to the latest complete Medicaid/CHIP month; the managed LTSS snapshot remains annual.

## Program interpretation

The four program landscape compares **Medicaid, Medicare, CHIP, and managed LTSS**, but these are not four disjoint groups. Medicare-Medicaid duals appear in both Medicare and Medicaid; managed LTSS is a Medicaid service delivery category and may include duals. Never sum the four columns as a unique enrollment figure. Renewal outcomes from the current CMS file are statewide, without these program splits. The annual managed LTSS table is older than the other three enrollment series, and many MLTSS-only cells are unavailable. The app displays missing cells rather than imputing zero.

## Enrollment forecast

Select a reporting month, Medicaid, CHIP, or Medicare, a model, and a 3–12 month horizon. The seasonal regression fits a linear time trend and calendar-month indicators by least squares using up to 36 months, requiring at least 30 reported months. The seasonal baseline uses the count from the same calendar month one year earlier plus the median year-over-year count change across the six latest available month pairs. It needs at least 20 reported months in the prior 24 months and at least four recent year-over-year pairs. Forecasts are floored at zero; missing states remain missing. The sidebar state selection controls the headline counts, forecast chart, forecast metrics, and forecast table. Selecting None shows the all-state view, which sums only forecastable jurisdictions. `medicare_history.csv` holds 37 CMS state-months from May 2023 through May 2026; `refresh_programs.py` updates it alongside the latest one-month Medicare snapshot.

The managed LTSS outlook is annual and separate from the monthly models. `mltss_history.csv` contains CMS years 2016–2024; a state and category get a linear trend only when the latest year is numeric and at least six annual observations are numeric. It projects the next three years after the latest CMS annual report. Many MLTSS-only cells are unavailable, so that category often has no outlook. A 2025 or 2026 annual estimate is still a projection even though those calendar years precede the latest monthly reporting month. Program reporting and definitions may change; this view has no validated prediction interval.

The displayed historical error is weighted absolute percentage error from up to six prior **one-month-ahead** predictions made using only information available at each origin. When a state is selected, the app compares that error with the other model and warns if the selected model performed worse. It is not an accuracy claim for 3–12 months ahead and is not a prediction interval. The monthly models do not incorporate federal or state policy, renewal outcomes, population shifts, or revisions to CMS preliminary reports. Treat results as exploratory planning figures. The state map measure selector is directly above the map and its adjacent ranking table.

## Current scope and next analyses

The September 24, 2026 snapshot has 5,559 distinct state-month rows; **May 2026** is the newest month with rows from all 51 jurisdictions in both sources. For that month, all 51 had a nonblank renewal denominator, procedural outcome, and enrollment in the extracted data. Its renewal data are original, and enrollment data preliminary: newer updates could change the values. We should verify material state footnotes before making comparative claims in an interview.

Next, verify effective-dated policy changes state by state, including which renewal cohorts were affected. Examine whether call center reporting is comparable and whether pending outcomes resolve after revisions. The enrollment baseline should be compared against additional holdout periods before operational use; no policy impact model has been trained in this version.
